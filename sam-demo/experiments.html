Making speech out of waves

One sentence — “I am a little robot. I come from 1982.” — rendered ten ways, all at the same knobs (pitch 64, speed 72, throat 128, mouth 128). Every clip is generated by Python in this folder; nothing is sampled except the SAM reference. Listen for which ones still sound like speech and which turn into noise, then read the formula under each.

Harmonicity = share of 0–10 kHz energy sitting within ±40 Hz of a harmonic of f0. Higher is more voice-like. Zero-crossings per second above ~2× f0 means high-frequency hash is dominating. Formant contrast = loudest spectral peak over the median level in 200–3500 Hz: low values mean the formants have merged into mush.

Listen

Cascade resonators

A sawtooth through F1 → F2 → F3 in series. Each resonator is normalised to unity gain at DC, so the product peaks as (F/bw)³: push the formants up and F3 takes over and the voice screams. This is what the synth did first.

y[n] = A·x[n] + B·y[n−1] + C·y[n−2]
C = −e^(−2π·bw·T)   B = 2e^(−π·bw·T)·cos(2π·f·T)   A = 1 − B − C

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 86.1% · zero-crossings 1152/s · formant contrast 178×

Parallel resonators (default now)

The same three resonators side by side, each with its own gain (1.0, 0.55, 0.30), summed. The formants keep their intended balance at any knob setting, and the bandwidths now scale with frequency (6% + 50 Hz) instead of being fixed at 70/110/180 Hz.

y[n] = Σ_k g_k · H_k(x[n])      H_k = 2-pole resonator at f_k, bw_k
bw_k = 0.06·f_k + 50 Hz

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 95.9% · zero-crossings 1077/s · formant contrast 77×

Glottal pulse source

The Rosenberg pulse instead of a sawtooth: 40% open phase, 16% return. Its spectrum rolls off faster than a saw's, which is the spectral tilt real voices have — less buzzy, more like a larynx.

g(t) = ½(1 − cos(π·t/Tp))          for 0 ≤ t < Tp
g(t) = cos(π·(t−Tp)/(2·Tn))        for Tp ≤ t < Tp+Tn
Tp = 0.4·T, Tn = 0.16·T

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 96.2% · zero-crossings 829/s · formant contrast 36×

Additive harmonics

No filters at all: sum every harmonic of f0, with each harmonic's amplitude read off the formant response at that frequency. Phases restart at every glottal pulse, so the wave is exactly periodic and does not acquire the hollow 'phasiness' of free-running oscillators.

x(t) = Σ_(k=1..K) A(k·f0)·sin(2π·k·f0·t)
A(f) = Σ_k g_k·f_k² / √((f_k² − f²)² + (f·bw_k)²)

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 85.8% · zero-crossings 969/s · formant contrast 64×

LPC resynthesis

Analyse the waveform into an all-pole filter (order 10) per 18 ms frame, then re-excite that filter with a pulse train plus noise. This is LPC-10, the 2.4 kbit/s vocoder: the formants survive because the filter carries them, the excitation does not.

x[n] ≈ Σ_(k=1..p) a_k·x[n−k]        →      1/A(z) driven by pulses + noise

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 94.1% · zero-crossings 985/s · formant contrast 39×

PSOLA, length ×1.6

Same waveform, 60% longer, same pitch and the same formants. Pitch marks are found by autocorrelation; windows of two periods are re-laid at scaled positions and overlap-added.

y = Σ_m w(t − m·Ts')·x(t − m·Ts)      Ts' = Ts·α

duration 6.17 s · harmonicity 92.0% · zero-crossings 834/s · formant contrast 75×

PSOLA, pitch ×1.3

Same length, f0 up 30%, formants unmoved — the thing naive resampling gets wrong (it moves formants by the same ratio, the chipmunk effect).

f0' = f0·β,   formant peaks unchanged

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 87.8% · zero-crossings 953/s · formant contrast 47×

Sinusoidal + noise (HNM)

Track every spectral peak frame by frame, give each partial a continuous phase, and keep the high band as noise. This is how a vocoder models sound: partials for the voice, noise for the breath.

x(t) = Σ_k A_k(t)·cos(φ_k(t)) + n(t)
φ_k(t) = φ_k(t−1) + 2π·f_k(t)/sr

duration 3.86 s · harmonicity 95.5% · zero-crossings 1663/s · formant contrast 127×

Naive resampling (the wrong way)

Playing the same samples 1.6× faster lengthens nothing: it raises pitch and formants together. Compare its spectrogram with psola-len.

y(t) = x(t/α)  →  every frequency ×α

The 1982 engine (reference)

The ported SAM renderer: three direct oscillators, phase reset every glottal pulse, 4-bit output. What the target sound actually is.

mux = (sin(F1)·A1 + sin(F2)·A2 + rect(F3)·A3)/32 + 128

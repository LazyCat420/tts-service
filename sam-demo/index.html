SAM demo — the real engine and a clean-room synth

Two synths in one folder. sam.py is a clean-room implementation: English text → phonemes → a buzzy glottal pulse train through three cascaded formant resonators, no SAM ROM data or port code. sam_engine/ is the opposite: a faithful port of the real 1982 engine — its tables, its three direct oscillators, its 4-bit output. Play both and hear what the port buys you.

Real SAM vs this folder's clean-room synth

Identical text, identical knobs (speed 72, pitch 64, throat 128, mouth 128). The difference is structural, not a matter of tuning: sam.py filters a sawtooth through three resonators, while the real engine sums three oscillators directly — sin(F1)·A1 + sin(F2)·A2 + rect(F3)·A3 — and resets their phase at every glottal pulse. That hard sync is what makes SAM's formants cut through and gives it its grit.

The port was checked against the reference engine byte for byte: 0 differing bytes on every case tested, and the reciter/parser output matches its phoneme stream and frame tables exactly. Its output is 8-bit PCM at 22.05 kHz, so the quantisation steps you can see in the spectrogram are SAM's own, not a defect.

Clear vs fuzzy — what changed

Same text, same synth. “Before” has no output band-limiting and the raw white-noise path (gain 1.4, bandwidth 1200 Hz); “after” band-limits at 80 Hz–4.8 kHz and tightens the noise (gain 0.35, bandwidth 700 Hz). Measured on the before clip: 6.11% of the energy above 5 kHz and 2192 sample steps over 25% of peak — that is the distorted hiss. After: 0.14% and 0.

What each knob does here

pitch   0-255  base f0 = 20 + pitch * 1.15 Hz      (64 → ~94 Hz)
speed   0-255  phoneme duration scale = 72/speed
throat  0-255  formant scale = (throat/128)^0.75   (all three formants)
mouth   0-255  F1 scale      = (mouth/128)^0.75    (apparent mouth size)

How it works

Knob semantics follow the real engine, measured on the port: mouth scales F1 only, throat scales F2 only, F3 is untouched (128 = unchanged). Formants are kept ordered (F1 < F2 < F3 < 0.45·SR) and their bandwidth grows with frequency — the fixed 70/110/180 Hz and the cascade's (F/bw)³ gain were what made the scaled-up presets scream.

text ──reciter──→ phonemes ──render──→ wav
                   |
                   +-- rules: digraphs, soft c/g, magic-e, r-coloured vowels,
                       plus a small pronunciation lexicon

buzz = polyBLEP sawtooth (or Rosenberg pulse) at f0, 18% declination
out  = buzz·voiced ─→ F1, F2, F3 in parallel, gains 1.0/0.55/0.30
                     (2-pole resonators, bw = 0.06·f + 50 Hz)
     + noise·fricative ─→ resonator at the fricative band

Formant values are adult-male phonetics tables; stops are rendered as closure + burst; diphthongs interpolate between two vowel targets. Target formants are smoothed with a one-pole filter (~15 ms) so phoneme transitions don't click.

Reproduce

python3 sam.py -wav out.wav -pitch 60 -speed 92 -throat 190 -mouth 190 "I am a robot"
python3 demo.py          # rebuilds audio/, spec/, index.html and README.md
